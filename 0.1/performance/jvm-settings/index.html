    JVM settings: bigger heaps, C2 and parallel GC¶

    Every other page runs the demo's JVMs deliberately small (-Xmx320m, C1-only JIT, serial GC) so that eight of them fit on a laptop. This page repeats the mode comparison on the 128 GB, 32-CPU test machine with 16 concurrent clients, to see how much of the result is the JVM settings rather than the architecture.

    Short answer. Giving each JVM more memory changes nothing measurable (1 GB and 2 GB agree, over five repetitions each); letting the JIT use C2 does most of the work, and the parallel collector adds a little throughput and a clearly better 99th percentile. Together they make the in-process shapes 37 to 59% faster, They leave the shapes that cross a process boundary almost where they were. The ordering of the shapes is unchanged, but the gap between the monolith and eight services widens from about 2.0x to 2.8x.

    What was run¶

    Both runs: scripts/perf.sh --modes all --cache both --orders 1500 --warmup 200 --concurrency 16, one run per configuration, differing only in DEMO_JAVA_OPTS.

    Run JVM flags per process Report
    C1 -Xmx2g -XX:+UseSerialGC -XX:TieredStopAtLevel=1 (the demo defaults, with 2 GB) report
    C2 -Xmx2g -XX:+UseParallelGC (full tiered JIT) report, split-dbs rerun

    Heap size and collector, repeated¶

    The comparison above changes the JIT and the collector together. To separate heap size and collector, scripts/jvm_matrix.sh ran every combination of 1 GB or 2 GB and serial or parallel GC, all with the full C2 JIT, five times each, interleaved (repetition 1 of all four, then repetition 2, and so on) so that drift on the machine hits every configuration alike. Each run is --modes all --cache on --orders 1500 --warmup 200 --concurrency 16, 20 runs in all. The tables are means over the repetitions, after dropping outliers: a run is dropped for a mode if its orders/s is more than two scaled MADs and more than 5% from the median of that mode's repetitions.

    • Heap size makes no measurable difference. 1 GB and 2 GB agree within the noise for every mode and collector, so 1 GB is enough for these JVMs at this load.
    • The collector barely moves throughput. Parallel is ahead of serial by 0 to 7%, most visibly on the in-process modes (modulith 437 against 470 at 1 GB). Medians are identical.
    • The collector shows in the tail. The 99th percentile is lower with the parallel collector in almost every row, for example 52 against 144 ms for modulith at 1 GB and 167 against 198 ms for microservices at 2 GB. The serial collector's stop-the-world pauses are a likely cause, but pauses were not measured, and the means include a few slow runs, so treat the size of the gap as indicative.
    • So the earlier jump was the JIT. Most of the 37 to 59% gain in the first comparison therefore came from C2, not from the collector or the heap.
    • The raw reports of the 20 runs are not published (about 800 lines each); the stamps are in .demo/jvm-matrix.txt of the run.

    What to take from it¶

    • In-process shapes gain the most. modulith, macro-modulith and loopback-codec rise from about 305 to 440 orders/s (cache off) and from about 330 to 465 (cache on). Their p99 also tightens, from about 160 ms to about 55 ms in most cases, which suggests the C1 runs' tails were compilation and collection pauses.
    • Process boundaries dominate once the JVM is fast. microservices and microservices-macro stay at about 140 (cache off) and 165 to 175 orders/s (cache on) under both settings: each order still pays about 20 calls over HTTP, and the code on either side of them was not the bottleneck.
    • The cost of splitting looks larger, not smaller. With C1, microservices ran at 50% of modulith; with C2 it runs at 36%. A faster JVM speeds up the work between the calls, so the calls become a bigger share.
    • The 16-client ceiling is higher than the 4-client one. The load page found saturation at about four clients with the small JVMs; with these settings 16 clients reach 465 orders/s on modulith, against 336 for four clients on the small JVMs. The two runs differ in both settings, so this does not separate the effect of clients from that of the JVM.

    Limits¶

    • One run per configuration, so differences below about 5 to 8% are noise.
    • The first comparison changes the JIT and the collector together; the repeated matrix varies only heap and collector, and C2 is inferred as the remainder.
    • Still one machine, loopback only, and a shared PostgreSQL server; see the method page.
    • The other pages keep their small-JVM numbers, so that their comparisons stay like for like.
